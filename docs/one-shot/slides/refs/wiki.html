RECURSIVE FEEDBACK LOOPS

#MEMORY#AGENTIC#SELF-OPTIMIZATION#TIER-4

01SUMMARY

A recursive feedback loop is how the learns from its own rulings. Every time the resolves a gate, the outcome — approved, denied, or reversed — is written back as a node and fed into the next decision. The vault stops being a passive archive and starts shaping what it decides next.

In the manual paradigm, an operator re-reads the same context on every review. That repetition is a cognitive tax, and a single missed precedent can send a decision the wrong way. The loop removes the tax by caching judgment the way caches computation.

02MECHANISM

Each ruling is decomposed during ingestion — see the pipeline at left — into entities and a relationship to prior nodes. When a new gate arrives, the queries the vault for the closest precedents before it reaches the decide phase. Confidence rises with every consistent outcome and drops sharply after a reversal, which is the system practicing — inferring the goal behind a request rather than matching its surface.

03TRADE-OFFS

Cached judgment is fast but it can calcify. The loop's guardrail is : any precedent older than its policy window is re-opened, not trusted blindly.

MANUAL REVIEW
  • Re-reads full context every pass
  • Precedent lives in the operator's head
  • One missed case flips the ruling
  • ~450ms per decision
FEEDBACK LOOP
  • Reuses cached judgment, re-checks on drift
  • Precedent lives in the vault, linked
  • Reversals lower confidence automatically
  • ~48ms per decision

04BENCHMARKS
